## What the brief says

### Raw material receiving — *a structure not in Parts 1–12*

> "When it comes they should enter the quantity for each material like 5 kg of something, or 30 bags like a cement bag but the material is not cement, or 1 wooden pallet. Each wooden pallet has a fixed number of bags and each bag has a fixed weight."

Materials arrive in a **three-level packaging hierarchy**:

```
1 Pallet  =  N Bags  =  N × W kg
```

The storekeeper should be able to type **"1 pallet"** and have the system work out the kilograms. Parts 1–12 have only a single `UnitId` per material and no packaging structure at all — so this requirement was lost entirely in the AI draft.

Needed on `Materials` (or a child table, since a material may come in more than one pack size):

| | |
|---|---|
| `BagsPerPallet` | e.g. 30 |
| `WeightPerBag` | e.g. 25 kg |
| `BaseUnitId` | kg |

Receiving then accepts a quantity **and** a pack level (pallet / bag / kg) and converts to the base unit for stock.

### Barcode every material unit — *far wider than Parts 1–12 allow*

> "The system will produce a barcode or QR code for every material unit like an id. We need to detect everything in the factory from the moment it enters, to be consumed or used, and what it produces."

The intent is that **incoming raw material units are barcoded too** — every bag of GPPS, not just rolls, bags of plates and pallets.

Part 8 §3 pushed "raw material containers" to a *future version*. That was the AI's decision, not the owner's. The v2 ERD hints at the real intent with `Materials.BarcodeTracked (bool)` — a per-material switch for exactly this.

This is a scope decision to confirm, not to assume — see **F11**.

### The three lines

> "The first line contains a mixer and extruder… produces a styrofoam roll by mixing specific quantities of raw materials. They have multiple mixtures for different types of rolls."
> "The second line converts the roll into plates. Each roll approximately produces 15 bags of plates; some rolls maybe produce 13 or 12 bags."
> "The third line is the recycler. In the second phase not all the roll was converted into plates, so the rest of the roll will enter the recycler to make a specific type of raw material to be used."

**Bags per roll varies: roughly 12–15.** The real form confirms it — 14, 12, 9, 12, 14.

This is separate from pallet capacity, which the form shows as 15 (pallets 1, 2 and 3 each hold exactly 15). Two similar numbers, two different things. Worth confirming they were not conflated in the draft — see **F12**.

### Shifts — **now fully specified**

> "The factory works in three shifts. The first shift is called A from 8am–4pm, the second shift is called B from 4[pm]–12[am], the third one is called C from 12am–8am."

| Shift | Start | End |
|---|---|---|
| A | 08:00 | 16:00 |
| B | 16:00 | 00:00 |
| C | 00:00 | 08:00 |

Matches the paper form exactly (shift A, 08:00–16:00).

**This resolves the night-shift problem in Q42.** Shift C runs 00:00–08:00, entirely inside one calendar day. No shift crosses midnight, so `ProductionDate` is never ambiguous.

### Why shifts exist

> "…to know which material is consumed in each shift and what is produced, whether the final plates or anything during the process like rolls with the quantities. So if there are any mistakes, to know who made the mistake."

Accountability is **by shift**, not by individual. Consistent with the answer that no one signs for material collection.

### Roll timing

> "We want to know when each roll is produced, in what hour and minute."

Confirms `ProducedAt` must be a full timestamp, and matches the **Out Time** field on the Roll Log app.

### Products

> "There are multiple types of styrofoam plates based on the mixture with different colors."

A product is **mixture (recipe family) + colour** — matching `NOR500` / `AB500` on the form, and the four pallet-matching attributes from F6.

## Why bags need individual barcodes — confirmed by the form's own numbers

The F6 answer is correct, and the paper form proves it arithmetically.

On 2 July, one green roll produced 14 bags and four yellow rolls produced 12 + 9 + 12 + 14 = 47 bags.

The pallet table shows: a 14-bag green group, pallets 1, 2, 3 with 15 bags each, and 2 loose bags. That is 45 + 2 = 47 yellow bags.

**So pallets 1, 2 and 3 each contain bags from more than one roll.** 15 does not divide 12, 9, 12 or 14 — the bags from consecutive rolls run together onto the same pallet.

Without a barcode per bag, there is no way to know which roll any bag on pallet 2 came from. With one, the chain holds. This is exactly the traceability the brief asks for, and it justifies the ~150,000 labels a year.
